# Notes on the Linden Orchard

The Linden orchard sits on a bend of the river, just south of the stone bridge. The parish planted it in 1924 after the flour mill closed. The soil is river silt over gravel, which drains fast and keeps the roots from standing in water.

## Trees

Three apples are grown on purpose. Ashmead's Kernel is the sharp one, picked for the press. Cox's Orange Pippin is the eating apple and is not pressed. Linden Gold is a seedling found beside the old dairy in 1951. It ripens late and tastes like honey and lemon peel.

There are also six perry pears along the west hedge. They are not named varieties. The parish just calls them the hedge pears.

## Harvest

Harvest is the second Saturday of October, even if the weather is poor. Volunteers meet at the gate at nine in the morning. Baskets live in the dairy loft. Children collect the windfalls, and adults use picking poles for the high branches.

Pressing happens the same afternoon in the old dairy. The press is a wooden rack-and-cloth press built in 1938. It is rinsed with well water, never soap. Juice that runs clear goes into two oak casks. Cloudy juice is boiled down into syrup for the winter market.

## The supper

The cider is never sold. At dusk the parish holds a harvest supper in the reading room and pours the previous year's cider, because the new juice is still sweet and cloudy. Bread is brought from the mill bakery. There is no ticket and no speech longer than five minutes.

People who cannot walk down the lane leave a basket request on the parish notice board. A volunteer delivers a bottle the next day. There is no phone list and no mailing list.

## Care of the ground

The grass is cut twice a year, in June and after harvest. Sheep are not allowed in the orchard because they strip the bark. Bees from the school hive visit in May. The parish does not spray. Wasps are tolerated until the week of harvest, when the jam jars go up.

If a tree fails, it is replaced in November with a maiden on a vigorous rootstock. The old stump is left as habitat. Linden Gold is grafted from wood kept in the dairy loft, labeled with the year it was cut.
